The Game

What Is Football?

Football, known as soccer in some countries, is a team sport played between two sides of eleven players, including a goalkeeper. The aim is simple: score more goals than the opponent by getting the ball into their goal, without using hands or arms (apart from the goalkeeper within their area).

A standard match lasts two halves of 45 minutes, with a short break between them. The referee may add extra minutes at the end of each half to make up for stoppages. If scores are level, the competition's rules decide whether the match is a draw or goes to extra time or a penalty shoot-out.

Football is popular because it needs little equipment: a ball and open space are enough. Children play it on streets, beaches, school grounds and village fields, as well as in professional stadiums.

The Ground

The Pitch

A football pitch is a rectangle, usually grass or artificial turf. Official match sizes vary within limits, but a typical professional pitch is about 105 metres long and 68 metres wide. Lines mark where the ball is in or out of play.

  • Halfway line and centre circle: mark where the match starts and restarts after a goal.
  • Penalty area: the large box around each goal, where the goalkeeper may use hands and where certain fouls lead to a penalty kick.
  • Goal area: the smaller box near the goal, used for goal kicks.
  • Corner arcs: where corner kicks are taken.

Youth and community football often uses smaller pitches, fewer players and smaller goals so that children get more touches and enjoy the game.

The Team

Player Positions and Formations

Each team has a goalkeeper and ten outfield players, usually grouped into defenders, midfielders and forwards. A formation describes how many players are in each line, counted from defence to attack. The diagram shows a common 4-4-2 shape: four defenders, four midfielders and two forwards.

Other formations such as 4-3-3 or 3-5-2 change the balance between attack and defence. Coaches pick shapes that suit their players and the opponent.

The Laws

Key Rules in Plain Language

The Laws of the Game are maintained by the sport's official lawmaking body and applied by referees worldwide. These summaries cover the basics only; exact wording and local competition rules can differ.

Kick-off & Goals

Play starts with a kick-off from the centre. A goal is scored when the whole ball crosses the goal line between the posts and under the crossbar.

Handball

Outfield players may not deliberately handle the ball. Only the goalkeeper may use hands inside their own penalty area.

Offside

A player is in an offside position if they are nearer the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent when the ball is played to them, with some exceptions, such as from a throw-in, goal kick or corner.

Fouls & Free Kicks

Careless, reckless or excessive-force challenges, holding and pushing are fouls, leading to a free kick. A foul by a defender inside their penalty area leads to a penalty kick.

Yellow & Red Cards

A yellow card is a caution. A red card sends a player off for serious offences, and the team continues with one fewer player.

Restarts

When the ball leaves the pitch, play restarts with a throw-in, goal kick or corner kick depending on how and where it went out.

Learn to Play

Core Football Skills

Passing

Use the inside of the foot for accuracy. Look up, pass firmly and move into space after passing.

Dribbling

Keep the ball close, use small touches and change direction and speed to beat a defender.

Shooting

Plant the standing foot beside the ball, keep your eyes on it and strike through the centre aiming at a corner.

Defending

Stay on your feet, stay goal-side of the attacker and be patient before committing to a tackle.

First Touch

Cushion the ball with a relaxed foot so it sits ready for your next action.

Goalkeeping

Stay balanced, watch the ball, use safe handling with hands behind the ball and communicate loudly.

Get Ready

Kit, Fitness and Simple Drills

Basic Equipment

  • Ball: choose the right size for the player's age.
  • Boots: match the studs to the surface – firm ground, artificial grass or indoor.
  • Shin guards: protect the lower legs and are required in most organised games.
  • Kit and socks: comfortable and breathable.
  • Goalkeeper gloves: improve grip and protect the hands.

Fitness Basics

Football needs stamina, speed, agility and balance. Jogging, short sprints, skipping and simple strength exercises such as squats and lunges are commonly used. Always warm up with light jogging and dynamic stretches, and cool down afterward.

Three Easy Drills

  1. Wall passes: pass against a wall and control the return with one touch.
  2. Cone dribbling: weave the ball through a line of cones using both feet.
  3. Target shooting: aim at corners of a goal or marked spots from short range.

Safety and Fair Play

Use shin guards, check the pitch for holes and debris, drink plenty of water and rest when tired. Heading the ball should be introduced carefully for young players following the guidance of their football association. Any suspected head injury needs immediate medical attention. Respect referees, opponents and teammates – fair play is as important as the result.

Quick Reference

Football Glossary

Dribble: to move with the ball under close control.
Clean sheet: a match in which a team does not concede a goal.
Hat-trick: three goals scored by one player in a match.
Through ball: a pass into space behind the defence.
Set piece: a restart such as a corner, free kick or throw-in.
Tackle: an attempt to win the ball from an opponent.
Nutmeg: passing the ball between an opponent's legs.
Stoppage time: extra minutes added for delays in play.
Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sportexa a betting or football-news website?

No. Sportexa is an educational sports resource. We do not offer betting, odds, predictions, tickets or live scores, and we do not sell products.

What age can children start playing football?

Many children enjoy simple ball games from around age four or five. Small-sided, fun-focused games work best in the early years, guided by a teacher or coach.

Do girls and women play football?

Yes. Girls' and women's football is played at school, club and international level around the world and continues to grow.

Why is the offside rule confusing?

It depends on the positions of players when the ball is played. The aim is to stop attackers waiting next to the goal. Watching examples and practising in small games helps.

Where can I find the official Laws?

The official Laws of the Game are published by the sport's lawmaking body and by national associations. Check their official websites for the latest version.
